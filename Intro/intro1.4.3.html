Time 1.4.3

Time is a broad concept and has been discussed in different fields through out the long human history [R.3]. Also the theatre journal: Theaterschrift picked time as its topic in one issue, Sabine Pochhammer wrote in the introduction Nevertheless, we cannot escape from it, for our existence is temporal; it is, according to Heidegger, being toward death. [6] Time is something strongly connected with our daily life, 'we are living in time'.

When we mention time in daily life, e.g. What time is it? Normally we refer to seconds, minutes, hours, days, months, or years and so on. Through out the generations, there have been many different ways to define time-units. Because of the new inventions of technology or discovery of new chemical element, our definition for the common unit of time is ever changing. A second is the base unit of time according to the International System of Units(SI), and many other time units, e.g. hours, develop from the duration of a second [R.4]. They have become a norm that regulates our sense of time in our life.

In the old times, many cultures had their own way to measure time and own calendar to record time. In Chinese culture, nowadays we still use many phrases and proverbs which indicate time but contains the sense of time from the past, e.g. by describing the position of the sun or the height of the water-level. Chinese New Year is always celebrated the first day of the year, the same as many other culture, but the Chinese calendar [R.5] considers this day to be around the beginning of February in the International common calendar. What I try to address here is that time is an abstract concept: it needs a certain frame or certain definition, in order to be discussed, to be aware of or to be used.

If there is no relativity, time has no influence on our perception. One minute itself does not give any temporal impression, there is nothing about it that is too short, too long, too fast or too slow. Only if I say, e.g., I read the whole page in one minute, but you took ten minutes, then a comparison between one and ten minutes is shown. Thus the one minute starts to have the effect on our cognition, my reading speed is quicker.

In this artistic research, especially on my practical projects, I tried to rethink and give a redefinition of the time-unit. For example, I let spectators experience the relatively of temporality through a sugar-falling installation which spectators can experience the relativity of temporality. The projects will be introduced in the chapter 2..
