Performer 1.4.4

I want to first make it clear among three words: performer, actor and character.

There have already been numerous research or academic definitions about the following three elements in the theatre field: performer, actor and character. I would like to reflect on what I have read during the research and explain the concept as I interpret them. Through these conceptualizations I hope that you, readers, understand why I choose (or did not choose) to use certain terms.

-Actor
Actors are humans who perform or act under a certain instruction or goal. They may or may not talk, but their act is the main way to convey the story. The quote from Intermediality in Theatre and Performance, written by Freda Chapple and Chiel Kattenbelt, .... This has led to a definition of theatre as a performance space in which unity of time is the principle structuring factor, and the actor is the dominant force, distinguishing himself from the static space in which he is moving. [7]

-Character
The characters are fictional roles in the performance. In Digital Theatre Words, [R.6] an on-going project for collecting theatre terminology by OISTAT, a character is a person represented in a drama or story. A character may or may not be carried out by a real human, e.g. a wrapped towel can be an infant in a theatre play.

-Performer
Everything can be a performer. A thing is performing means that something's status is changing (position, colour, shape, or temperature, etc.) and the change should relate to a meaningful reason in the whole performance.
