Three Elements in This Research: Time, Performer and Space.

Time, performer and space are three important and unavoidable elements in performance art. In the book Postdramatic Theatre, written by Hans-Thies Lehmann, in the chpater Performance, he writes With reference to the notion and practice of 'Concept Art' (as it flourished especially around 1970), postdramatic theatre can be seen as an attempt to conceptualize art in the sense that it offers not a representation but an intentionally unmediated experience of the real (time, space, body): Concept Theatre. [4] The three are main subjects in this artistic research as well, only I use the term performer, rather than actor in order to elaborate on the discussion between the human performer and the non-human performer.

Because of my architectural background, designing a space to me means creating a space for letting things happen. Radivoje Dinulović in his article The Ideological Function of Architecture in the Society of Spectacle wrotes function of instead of function in architecture needs to be observed as a dominant value. Furthermore, the word 'function' should be understood as pluralia tantum, or even material noun. As photography does not reach art through painting but through theatre (Barthes), architecture reaches art through its functions and not through visuality. [5] It doesn't really matter the space is a public square, a private living room or a stage in theatre. Furthermore, in order to start the design, as a spatial designer, I must first know who will be the users, and what event will take place and when. Users, time and event are prioritized in my designing process, and then space will be formed according to those elements. Therefore in this research, I do not discuss the space in performance works from the beginning, yet through analyzing the two elements, time and performer in each work, I believe the notion of space across the works in made explicit or at least is sensed from the analysis.
