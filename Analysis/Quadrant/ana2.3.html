THE QUADRANT AND THE ANALYSIS OF WORKS 2.

Y-Axis 2.3

One-direction way
Forking Paths

The Y-axis is about performer: the object(s) that are changing its status in order to tell the story in the work. (Diagram 3.)

At the bottom of this axis is Human Performer and on the top is Non-human Performer. From the bottom towards the top, there are four transitions, (a.)acting according to the script, (b.) moving because of the interaction, (c.) changing passively and (d.) changing the status according to its own rule.

(a.) Acting according to the script
The classical way of acting. The performer reads or memorizes the script and presents it in his or her own way.

(b.)Moving because of interaction
The performers have certain movement because of an interaction through out the dramaturgic process. A new relation is created among the performers and thus triggers another movement that is not directly generated by the script.

(c.) Changing passively
If the (b.) means that the performer takes the initiative action to change something in the performance, then there might be some other elements affected by this changing. For example, I grab a cup, the cup is grabbed by me. Its position is changed from standing on a table to being hold in my hand.

(d.)Changing the status according to its own rule
The performer changes according to its own rule. It is difficult to estimate what will be its next step or when it will finish. It has its own rhythm and rule. For example, the day light generates the shadow and moves its own orbit during the day, it is a natural phenomenon, we cannot change anything about it. Another more extreme example, I walk in a busy street, many people pass by me and I cannot control their direction. The only thing I can do is to change my route, in order to avoid being blocked by people. The people here become an element with its own rule.
