THE QUADRANT AND THE ANALYSIS OF WORKS 2.

X-Axis 2.2

One-direction way
Forking Paths

The X-axis is about time: the time that the spectators perceive in the performative works. (Diagram 2.)

From the left side of this axis is Real Time and Fictive Time at the right side. There are four transitions on this continuum (a.) one layer of time, (b.) Collage of time, (c.) Confusion of time duration and (d.) multiple layers of time

(a.) One layer of time
There is only one-direction story line in the performance without repeating, overlapping or any form of changing its temporality. The spectators are brought into this story and follow the whole dramaturgy which is created by the theatre group or the artist. For example, You sit in the theatre for 2 hours for a performance, it’s a story about a woman’s whole life. It is presented in a narrative way, from her birth to her death. This is what I mean about One layer of time.

(b.)Collage of time
When the theatre group or artist presents their work by representing or recomposing the elements which are collected at different periods of time. The essence of time has not been changed, the performance is presented as a collage of time. The work Tagfish [P.5] that I’ve selected to discuss in this research is a performance that presented by series of interview videos which were taken at different times and different places.

(c.) Confusion of sense of time duration
The theatre group or artist tries to twist the audience’s sense of duration. Imagine a situation where you are sitting in a black box and there is only one actor on stage. She/he is doing nothing but only walking in circles at the same speed around the stage. Maybe you will feel like it’s been half an hour but actually only 5 minutes have passed. The repeating movement makes you feel confused about duration.

(d.)Multiple layers of time
Spectators experience and perceive different layers of time during the performance. In order to achieve this state, there should be one layer of time as a base upon which more are built. The German theatre researcher Hans-Thies Lehmann in his book Postdramatic Theatre writes The unity creates a continuity that is meant to make invisible any split between fictive time and real time. [1] I quite agree with this quote of Lehmann. Let’s take the example I mentioned above, the one where the only actor is walking in circles for the whole performance. Imagine again, if your phone or watch is taken away by a crew before you enter the black box, the only thing you have with you to indicate time is the ticket which writes that the performance will last 2 hours. Ok, you find the seat and the show starts, during the whole performance, you cannot know how much time is still left. You feel like floating in the air, very uncertain about time. Maybe you even feel it’s been 4 hours when the show is finished. Until you get your watch back, you realize only 1 hour has passed. I consider that the performance has ended at the moment you look at the clock, not in the theatre hall. Because of the action of looking at the clock, you receive two layers of time, 1 hour in reality but 4 hours in experience.
